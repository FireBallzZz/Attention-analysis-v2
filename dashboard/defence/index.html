Hybrid classroom attention analysis

A six-stage system that finds every student in classroom video, measures pose, gaze and behaviour, scores attention over time and suggests a teaching response when attention drops — evaluated honestly on classrooms it has never seen.

Pipeline

STAGE 1
Video input

Any classroom video, resampled to 10 fps.

STAGE 2
Detect & track

YOLO11-Pose (COCO) + tracking: one ID per student.

STAGE 3
Modalities

Head pose, L2CS gaze (Gaze360), SCB behaviour (reading, writing, hand-raise, phone).

STAGE 4
Attention model

Each student's movement relative to the class over 1.6 s. Hard evidence (phone, sleeping) lowers it; confident SCB reading/writing protects it. Chosen because it transfers between classrooms.

STAGE 5
Drop detector

Sustained (<40 for 3 s) or sudden (−20 in 2 s) drops, 8 s cooldown.

STAGE 6
Recommendation

Rule-based teaching suggestion per alert (LLM option off).

Contributions

  • Working end-to-end pipeline on real classroom video, real-time capable on a laptop GPU.
  • Human-labelled HIGH/LOW attention data for three classrooms.
  • Leakage-free evaluation: split by person and by classroom, epoch and threshold chosen on held-out people only.
  • Finding: camera-relative head/gaze cues flip meaning between rooms, and learned models recognise who a student is rather than attention; class-relative motion is the cue that transfers.

    Same image model, inside each video. It can follow a student it has already seen, but it cannot judge a new person.

    Does DAiSEE help?

    Same BiGRU, unseen classrooms, mean over rooms.

    Pre-training helps a little; adding DAiSEE clips to training does not. DAiSEE is webcam video of one learner (5.7% LOW), not a classroom.

    Does adding DAiSEE or SCB to the score help?

    Live-pipeline components, unseen classrooms, mean over rooms. Each component normalised within its own video.

    The DAiSEE-only model applied to each student individually is at chance on classroom students; SCB behaviour cues flip between rooms. Both are shown per student in the Live demo, but the score uses motion only.

    Other datasets considered

    • SCB (classroom behaviour): used for behaviour detection and image features; its cues flip between rooms, so they are not used to score attention directly.
    • FER2013 / RAF-DB: not used — faces at classroom distance are 30–60 px and emotion is not attention.
    • DIPSER (54 students, 51 h, per-second attention ratings in a real classroom): the best fit for future work; access needs an application.
    • Published online-to-classroom transfer reaches about 71% (binary), in line with these results.

    How to state it at the defence: "In a classroom it has never seen, with no setup, the system reaches about 59% balanced accuracy, better than the neural model's 52%, because it uses the one cue that transfers: movement relative to the class. After a short labelled calibration of the teacher's own class it reaches about 71% balanced and 81% accuracy on the rest of the lesson."

    Key finding: cues measured from the camera do not transfer

    Head pose and gaze are measured relative to the camera. Attention means looking at the teacher or board — and the board sits somewhere different in every room. A cue that means "attentive" in one classroom means "inattentive" in another.

    Implication

    The final model ignores camera-relative head/gaze channels. The principled fix — future work — is a "looking at the board" feature: mark the board once per camera and measure each student's head direction relative to it.

    Data & labels

    Only human labels are used. Each 16-frame window (1.6 s) of a student is one sample; HIGH = attentive, LOW = not attentive.

    Labelling rule

    • HIGH looking at teacher/board/screen, writing or reading lesson material (head down is fine), raising a hand. Short glances away count as HIGH.
    • LOW lasting more than ~2 s: phone, head turned far away, head on desk / sleeping, chatting, playing with objects.
    • Unsure → HIGH. Only clear cases are LOW.

    Pretrained components (not trained here)

    • COCO via YOLO11-Pose — student detection and keypoints.
    • Gaze360 via L2CS-Net — gaze direction.
    • SCB-dataset — classroom behaviour (hand-raise, reading, writing…).
    • DAiSEE — pre-training of the attention network.

    Corrections to earlier results

    An internal audit found leakage and metric errors in the earlier report. Every number in this dashboard comes from the corrected, leakage-free evaluation.

    Limitations, future work & likely questions

    Limitations

    • Three labelled classrooms and short clips — confidence intervals are wide.
    • Attention is inferred from visible behaviour; it is not a measure of learning or cognition.
    • Head/gaze measured relative to the camera; removed in the final model.
    • One room's "non attentive" clip is staged; acted inattention may differ from real.
    • Recommendations are rule-based; the LLM option is not enabled.

    Future work

    • Board-relative gaze feature (one click per camera).
    • More classrooms rather than more clips of the same room.
    • Model on student image crops instead of 32 hand-made features.
    • Second annotator and Cohen's κ for label reliability.
    • Privacy: on-device processing, no identities stored.
